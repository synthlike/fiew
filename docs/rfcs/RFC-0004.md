# Add durable Discoveries and explanatory Trails alongside Reviews

## Summary

Expand fiew from a review-specific conversation workspace into a read-first workspace supporting two distinct human-agent workflows:

- discoveries for durable code discovery and understanding; and
- reviews for evaluating changes and controlling approval.

Both workflows use anchored conversations and may contain explanatory Trails, but they retain different lifecycle and authority semantics. Reviews remain approval-oriented. Discoveries remain knowledge-oriented.

## Motivation

Review threads already provide a useful way to ask an agent questions about changed code, but their Git-diff anchors, Open/Resolved lifecycle, and approval consequences make them unsuitable for general code discovery. A developer should be able to ask durable questions about any repository source without creating a review or pretending that the question is a blocking concern.

Trails also describe code flows useful during discovery and review. Their v0.2 review-local ownership is an intentionally narrow release boundary, not a sufficient long-term domain model. Agents could explain a flow more effectively by proposing an ordered, annotated Trail in a reply rather than flattening paths and source locations into comment text.

## Requirements and constraints

- A repository may contain durable named discoveries independently of reviews.
- Each repository may have one explicit current discovery and one explicit current review at the same time.
- Human start and open actions control the current-discovery pointer. Agent show and reply operations never change it. Missing, malformed, or dangling current state fails rather than selecting the newest discovery.
- Discovery threads attach to repository-relative source ranges. External files are excluded initially.
- Only a human creates discovery and review threads.
- Agents may read the selected container and append replies only through constrained command interfaces.
- Discovery thread lifecycle is Open, Answered, or Archived. Only a human marks a thread Answered, reopens it, or archives it.
- Anchor validity is independent of conversation lifecycle and is Current or Outdated.
- fiew automatically re-evaluates anchors through stored-location validation and constrained exact-context relocation. It never archives a thread automatically because of age or anchor loss.
- Outdated conversations retain their complete history and may become Current again after exact re-anchoring or explicit human reassignment.
- Archiving a discovery with Open threads requires explicit confirmation and preserves every thread's lifecycle and anchor validity for restoration.
- Reviews remain specialized containers with diff/file anchors, Open/Resolved lifecycle, Outdated approval blocking, and reviewer-controlled approval.
- Discovery threads do not become review concerns implicitly. A human may explicitly create a linked review concern while preserving separate Answered and Resolved lifecycles.
- Bookmarks remain private single-location navigation artifacts.
- Trails are ordered, explanatory navigation artifacts distinct from threads, concerns, and bookmarks.
- A Trail may be attached explicitly to a discovery or review without affecting review approval.
- Unattached Trails remain private. An attached Trail is included only in that container's agent-facing projection.
- A human may create and save a Trail directly.
- An agent may propose a Trail only while replying to an existing human-created thread. The proposed Trail belongs to the same discovery or review and the reply carries a structured attachment reference to it.
- fiew validates every agent-proposed repository-relative path, range, bound, and captured source context before storing the proposal.
- Agent-proposed Trails begin as Proposed. A human may accept or archive them.
- Trail authorship is immutable. Human acceptance records endorsement separately without replacing agent provenance.
- Trail points carry ordered repository-relative anchors, captured exact context, independent Current/Outdated validity, and short point-specific explanations.
- Archival retains threads, comments, Trails, references, provenance, and acceptance history. Historical structured references must continue to resolve to archived artifacts.
- Existing v0.1 and v0.2 review, bookmark, and Trail contracts remain unchanged until a later accepted specification explicitly supersedes them.
- Storage remains private, schema-versioned, atomically replaced, recoverable through validated backup, and protected by future-schema refusal.

## Non-goals

- Making discovery threads affect review approval.
- Allowing agents to create threads, mark them Answered or Resolved, accept Trails, archive artifacts, or change current-container pointers.
- Automatically archiving conversations based on age, anchor loss, or agent claims.
- Guessing anchor relocation through fuzzy similarity.
- External-file discovery anchors in the initial model.
- Treating Trails as inferred execution traces, call graphs, or correctness evidence.
- Exposing every private bookmark or unattached Trail to agents.
- Retrofitting these semantics into `fiew.review/v1` or `fiew.trail/v1` without an explicit compatibility decision and specification.
- Selecting exact storage paths, schema versions, command names, or key bindings in this RFC.

## Open questions

None at the product-model boundary. Storage evolution, compatibility, command syntax, structured attachment representation, and detailed interaction behavior require follow-up decisions and specification work.

## Options

### Option A: Keep conversations and Trails review-local

Retain the current model. This avoids new containers and schemas, but requires developers to create reviews for ordinary code questions and keeps explanatory Trails unavailable to discovery agents. Review approval semantics remain overloaded as a general conversation mechanism.

### Option B: Replace reviews with one generic conversation container

Use one container and thread type for discovery and review. This maximizes structural reuse but conflates Answered with Resolved, source anchors with diff anchors, and durable understanding with approval gates. Generic lifecycle flags would obscure rather than remove the domain distinction.

### Option C: Add discoveries beside reviews and share explicit artifact concepts

Keep discoveries and reviews as distinct containers. Reuse conservative anchored conversations, append-only comments, command-mediated agent access, provenance, and Trail navigation where their semantics agree. Keep lifecycle, anchoring, visibility, and approval behavior explicit where they differ.

### Option D: Store standalone repository-wide discovery threads without discoveries

This is simpler initially, but routine agent handoff would expose unrelated historical questions or require thread-ID exchange. It also lacks a durable unit for grouping, restoring, and archiving a coherent discovery effort.

## Recommendation

Choose Option C.

Named discoveries give code discovery a bounded durable context while preserving review as a specialized approval workflow. Shared artifact concepts reduce interaction inconsistency without forcing unlike lifecycles into one schema. Human-created threads retain the current authority boundary. Agent-proposed Trails add a structured way to explain code flows while Proposed and Accepted states preserve human control over durable knowledge.

Treat the v0.2 review-local Trail model as compatible existing behavior, not as the final ownership boundary. Follow-up design should determine how future repository Trails attach to discoveries and reviews, how structured comment references remain resolvable, and whether new schemas coexist with or migrate existing private records.

## Resolution

Accepted by decision owner synthlike.

Choose Option C. Keep durable named Discoveries and Reviews distinct while sharing explicit anchored-conversation, comment, provenance, re-anchoring, and Trail concepts where their semantics agree. Discovery remains knowledge-oriented with human-controlled Open, Answered, and Archived lifecycle. Review remains approval-oriented with distinct concern and approval behavior. Use Discovery and Review as the canonical container names.
